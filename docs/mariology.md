# Mary, Theotokos, and the communion of saints

Definitions are in [mariology_concepts.dip](../dip/mariology_concepts.dip), citations in [extension_sources.dip](../dip/extension_sources.dip), and qualified relations in [extension_edges.dip](../dip/extension_edges.dip). These concepts appear in the ordinary graph as well as the guided tours, with **Mary & divine motherhood** and **Communion & intercession** color groups.

## Theotokos and Christ

**Theotokos**, God-bearer or Mother of God, is expressed in Latin as **Dei Genetrix**. The concept `divine_motherhood` concerns the person whom Mary bore. The `mary_mother_son` edge names the eternal Son as her son, with the condition **according to his human birth**. It does not assert that Mary produces divinity or is mother of the Father or the Holy Spirit. Aquinas explains this distinction in [ST III, q. 35, a. 4](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4035.htm#article4).

The model adds `incarnation` and `human_nature` to make this intelligible: the Son assumes a complete human nature without a second human person or a mixture of natures. See [CCC 464–469](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1J.HTM). Mary is classified as a human creature, not a divine person.

## Marian teachings and historical precision

| Concept | Principal basis | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Perpetual virginity | ST III, q. 28; CCC 496–507 | Concerns Mary before, in, and after Christ's birth |
| Immaculate Conception | CCC 490–493 | Concerns Mary's own conception and preservation through Christ's merits |
| Assumption | CCC 966 | Mary taken body and soul into glory at the completion of earthly life |
| Spiritual motherhood and intercession | Lumen gentium 60–62 | Entire dependence on Christ's mediation |

Aquinas's position in [ST III, q. 27, a. 2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4027.htm#article2) differs from the later definition of the Immaculate Conception. The graph cites Church teaching for the dogma and the tour explicitly marks the historical distinction. The Immaculate Conception is also distinguished from the virginal conception of Christ.

The Assumption record follows the defined formula without assigning a manner of Mary's death. It connects Marian glorification to Heaven and the existing beatific vision concept. See [CCC 963–975](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2C.HTM).

## Asking Mary and the saints to pray

The ontology distinguishes:

- **Invocation** (*invocatio*): asking a saint for intercession.
- **Intercession** (*intercessio*): the saint's prayer to God for others.
- **Dulia**: honor of God's holy servants.
- **Hyperdulia**: the singular veneration of Mary.
- **Latria**: adoration owed to God alone.

Aquinas distinguishes addressing God as the giver of a good from asking saints to obtain it by prayer in [ST II-II, q. 83, aa. 4 and 11](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3083.htm). He denies latria to Mary in [ST III, q. 25, a. 5](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4025.htm#article5). Explicit graph relations preserve these distinctions; they are not differences of emotional intensity.

The saints' intercession depends on Christ's unique mediation. Communion includes both those in glory and those undergoing purification, with invocation of the blessed distinguished from suffrages for the dead. See [CCC 954–958](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2B.HTM) and [Lumen gentium 60–62](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html).
