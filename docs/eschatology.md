# Heaven, Hell, Purgatory, and resurrection

The ontology now includes the last things as cited concepts, with Latin names and a shared **Last things & resurrection** display group. Definitions are in [eschatology_concepts.dip](../dip/eschatology_concepts.dip); their relations and qualifications are in [extension_edges.dip](../dip/extension_edges.dip). Passage records are in [extension_sources.dip](../dip/extension_sources.dip).

| Concept | Latin | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Heaven | *caelum* | Definitive communion with God, including the beatific vision |
| Hell | *infernus* | Definitive exclusion from blessed communion, not the general abode of the dead |
| Purgatory | *purgatorium* | Final purification of the saved, ordered toward Heaven |
| Human death; separated soul | *mors hominis; anima separata* | Separation of soul and body; not the destruction of the soul |
| Particular judgment | *iudicium particulare* | Judgment of each person at death |
| Last Judgment | *iudicium universale* | Universal judgment following resurrection |
| Resurrection of the dead | *resurrectio mortuorum* | Reunion with one's own body; includes both just and unjust |
| Purification; temporal punishment | *purificatio a peccato; poena temporalis peccati* | Distinguished from forgiven guilt and from eternal punishment |
| Suffrages for the dead | *suffragia pro defunctis* | Prayer and ecclesial acts of charity for the departed |
| New heaven and new earth | *caelum novum et terra nova* | Final renewal of humanity and creation |

## Purgatory tour

**Purgatory, forgiveness, and the hope of heaven** is tagged for dialogue with Protestant and Evangelical Christians and for Catholic formation. Its six scenes cover death and judgment, Heaven and Hell, forgiven guilt and remaining consequences, purification of the saved, prayer for the departed, and resurrection.

The distinction between forgiveness and remaining disorder is grounded in Aquinas, [ST III, q. 86, aa. 5–6](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4086.htm). The Church's confession of Purgatory and its practice of suffrages are cited to [CCC 1030–1032](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_three/article_12/iii_the_final_purification,_or_purgatory.html). The tour identifies the Catholic use of 2 Maccabees and acknowledges that many Protestant traditions do not receive it as canonical.

The [Summa Supplement, q. 71](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/5071.htm#article6) and [Appendix II on Purgatory](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/7001.htm) are labeled as posthumously compiled or editorial material drawn from Aquinas's earlier work. They are not represented as a completed portion of the Summa written by Aquinas.

## Model boundaries

Edges express cited distinctions, conditions, and relations. They do not classify the eternal destiny of an individual. Purgatory is not a third permanent destination or a new opportunity to choose salvation after death. No duration, temperature, physical location, or mechanism is encoded.

The separation and reunion edges between soul and body have explicit conditions: death and resurrection respectively. Resurrection is not equated with universal beatitude. The existing `beatific_vision` concept is reused, and the new concepts connect to human nature, grace, sin, Christ, the Eucharist, and the communion of saints.
