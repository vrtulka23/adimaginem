# Guided tours

Open **Guided tours** at the top of the graph's left sidebar. The catalogue has descriptions, audience and topic filters, and 26 tours with 91 scenes. The filters combine: a tour must match both selections. All tours are available to everyone; audience tags describe invitations to dialogue, not an assessment of a visitor's beliefs.

## Pick a question

For short introductions to common questions, select **Common questions** in the catalogue's topic filter. Each of these four tours has three scenes:

- **Does faith mean giving up reason?** Explore how faith engages thought and how reason relates to revelation.
- **Is Christ sacrificed again at every Mass?** Explore sacramental memorial and Christ's presence.
- **Why confess to a priest if God forgives?** Follow the connection between God's forgiveness, priestly ministry, and personal conversion.
- **Do Catholics put tradition above the Bible?** Explore how revelation is handed on and how Church teaching serves it.

The scenes use existing cited concepts and relations. Their explanations include the distinctions that a diagram alone cannot convey.

## Follow a tour

Select **Start tour**, then **Previous** or **Next**. Each scene shows its question, explanation, qualification, and source links. Concepts remain clickable, and the camera can rotate and zoom. Saved coordinates seed a three-dimensional force layout. Attraction, repulsion, and icon/arrow collision clearance relax each scene; a reproducible depth spread prevents coplanar starting arrangements. The camera fits the result after settling. Labels remain visible.

**Main concepts are emphasized; supporting concepts remain quieter.** Supporting icons and their labels are translucent, and supporting relations use their normal colors and widths. Click any supporting icon to inspect its definition and citations. The original set of tours includes thirty-eight scenes with additional connective context and 47 supporting concept placements; the common-question tours also include the endpoints needed by their selected relations. The original focus of each step is preserved.

The connections are explicitly selected from existing cited ontology edges. They are not generated from graph proximity. Examples include the soul and appetitive power between faith/intellect and charity/will; intellect between its speculative and practical uses; and saints in Heaven between invocation and intercession. Supporting sources appear with the other step citations.

**Exit tour** or **Finish tour** restores the exploration view, including filters, icon size, selection, connection search, camera, and the saved layout. **Icon size**, **Reheat layout**, **Restore starting layout**, and **Reset graph view** work during a tour. Restore and Reset restart the current scene from its authored coordinates and depth spread. Nodes can be dragged; selecting one does not pin it. Exploration filters remain inactive until exit.

## Sidebar organization

The left sidebar has three keyboard-accessible accordions: **Guided tours**, **Explore**, and **Concept connections**. Opening one closes the others. Explore opens initially. During a tour, the active scene remains inside Guided tours, and ordinary exploration controls are temporarily hidden. Exiting restores the prior accordion state as well as the graph.

## Sources and interpretation

Tour prose is authored in DIPL. It is an editorial explanation of the cited passages, stored editorial text rather than prose generated during browsing or a new ontology relation. Every displayed line references an existing sourced edge. Concept placement is a reading aid: spatial separation does not imply parts in God, and proximity is not a measure of causal strength.

The Trinity scenes use Aquinas's *Summa theologiae* I, questions [27](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1027.htm), [34](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1034.htm), [37](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1037.htm), and [42](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1042.htm), together with the [Catechism's account of the Trinity](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_2_the_father.html). They distinguish one divine essence from personal distinction, and eternal origin from temporal beginning.

The creation tour distinguishes creation from natural change and from the question of a first moment. See *Summa theologiae* [I, q. 45](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1045.htm), [I, q. 46, a. 2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1046.htm#article2), and [Catechism 301](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_4_the_creator.index.html). The scenes illustrate claims and arguments; the diagrams do not prove them.

The tours have `review_status = "draft"`, meaning that our editorial encoding awaits scholarly review. This does not qualify the authority of the underlying texts. See the project's [scholarly and Catholic method](method.md).

The dedicated [sin and reconciliation](sin-and-angels.md#sin-intellect-and-reconciliation-tour), [last things](eschatology.md), and [Mary and the saints](mariology.md) guides document the added ontology and its interpretive boundaries. All scenes use the same canonical concepts visible in ordinary exploration.

The common-question tours draw on Aquinas's [account of faith in the intellect (II–II, q. 4, a. 2)](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3004.htm#article2), [the Eucharistic sacrifice (III, q. 83, a. 1)](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm#article1), and [sacramental absolution (III, q. 84, a. 3)](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4084.htm#article3), alongside the Catechism and [*Dei Verbum*, §§9–10](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html). Each scene carries its precise source records in DIPL. See [editorial review and contributions](review-and-contributions.md) for how these explanations were prepared and can be corrected.

## Authoring in DIPL

The canonical files are in [`dip/tours/`](../dip/tours/). Three map schemas separate reusable catalogue information from individual scenes and coordinates:

| Map | Contents |
|---|---|
| `graph_tours` | Title, description, audience and topic tags, ordered step IDs, framing note, review status |
| `graph_tour_steps` | Tour ID, title, question, narrative, qualification, all visible source/concept/edge IDs, highlighted concept/edge subsets, focus concept, camera and look-at coordinates |
| `graph_tour_positions` | Step ID, concept ID, and explicit initial `x`, `y`, `z` coordinates |

These are presentation maps outside `ontology`. Sources remain in `ontology.sources`. No additional philosophical relation is inferred from tour membership or geometry.

For example, an existing position is declared as:

```dipl
graph_tour_positions[creation_duration_god]
  step_id = "creation_duration"
  concept_id = "god"
  x = -8.0
  y = 3.0
  z = 0.0
```

To add a tour:

1. Add a catalogue record in `catalogue.dip`, applying the existing map schema. Use stable IDs and an ordered `step_ids` list.
2. Add scene records in a new `.dip` file and import it in `dip/DIPfile`. Cite authoritative passages in each scene's `source_ids`; add source records only when needed.
3. Select existing visible concepts and edges in `concept_ids` and `edge_ids`. Both endpoints of every edge must appear in the scene. Use `highlighted_concept_ids` and `highlighted_edge_ids` for the subsets emphasized by the step; other visible entries provide context. The focus concept must be highlighted. Every supporting concept must connect to the highlighted concepts by visible cited relations. Preserve direction and qualifications, and include the supporting passages in `source_ids`.
4. Give every selected concept one position for that scene. Set a camera position and look-at target; the default camera is `(0, 0, 32)` looking at the origin.
5. Write a concise question, explanation, and qualification. Avoid attributing a belief to every member of an audience, and identify distinctions that the diagram could otherwise obscure.

The shared viewer validator rejects missing citations, unknown or out-of-scene edges, invalid highlighted subsets, disconnected supporting concepts, repeated or orphan steps, missing/duplicate positions, nonfinite coordinates, and invalid camera targets. Supporting positions are collected in [`context_positions.dip`](../dip/tours/context_positions.dip). `tour_icon_size`, `tour_transition_ms`, `tour_context_opacity`, and `tour_depth_ratio` are configured in `dip/graph_viewer.dip`; reduced-motion preferences disable camera animation. `tour_depth_ratio` scales the initial depth perturbation by icon size (zero retains the authored depth). Tour settling reuses the shared DIPL attraction, repulsion, collision, and cooling controls. Projection can still produce line crossings; rotating the graph exposes its depth.

## Running and checking

Tours use the same implementation in [local Python and static Astro modes](../tools/README.md). Restart the Python server after DIPL edits. Rebuild the static site after DIPL or frontend edits; the generated data includes all tours and the shared validator.

```sh
node --test tools/guided_tours.test.mjs
./setup.sh --check
```

The unit checks cover scene consistency. The site checks verify the generated data and routes; both running modes use the same native loader. Neither check replaces theological review.
